ROUTE CARD · DEPART 10 OCT · 2 MOTORCYCLES + 1 SUV · 14 DAYS

Up the Srinagar side, and decide about Manali in Leh

The ascent is settled for acclimatisation reasons, not weather ones. The descent stays open as a live choice made on day ten, on advisories rather than forecasts.

Ascent, committed Descent, conditional First safe overnight coming down

Why this way round

Srinagar wins on acclimatisation before it wins on anything else. It steps you from 1,585 m to 2,676 m at Kargil and only then to 3,500 m at Leh, across two sleeps. The Manali side puts you at Sarchu, around 4,290 m, on your second night out of Manali — that is the single most common way riders get altitude sickness on this circuit.

Weather is the second argument, not the first. Manali–Leh is not shut in mid-October: it closed for winter on 20 November 2025, and on similar dates in 2023 and 2022. What makes 10 October marginal is not the calendar but the chance of one weather system arriving while you are between Darcha and Upshi.

Two-wheeler restrictions past Darcha belong to the opening season, not the closing one. In late May 2025 bikes were kept off the road for the first couple of weeks while snow walls were cut back. That rule does not reappear in October.

What the support vehicle changes

Most of the standard case against Manali in October is really a case against riding it unsupported. The 365 km fuel gap from Tandi to Karu exists in July too, and jerry cans close it. Punctures, a rider who stops coping with the cold, an early AMS presentation — all of these become manageable when there is a heated cabin and a spare seat moving with you. Keep the SUV behind the bikes, not ahead.

Two ways in, by the clock

Moving time only — no tea, no photographs, no punctures, no convoy waits. Assume a riding window of 8 am to 6 pm, so anything past about eight and a half hours is already eating into the dark.

Both tabs are the same closed loop — Kurukshetra, Manali, Leh, Srinagar, home — ridden in opposite directions. So the kilometres and the hours are identical by definition, and totals tell you nothing. What differs is where in the trip the hard legs fall.

None of the above is a booking. In October availability changes week to week, so ring each halt yourself and ask two questions: is the property open on your dates, and is there working room heating. Get Nubra, Pangong and your Jispa night confirmed before you leave home — those three have the fewest fallbacks. Everywhere else you can improvise. Carry cash; card machines fail past Kargil.

Day ten, in Leh

Depart 10 October, so day ten is 19 October.

Do not settle the return route before you leave home. Settle it here, on the rest day in Leh, with four days of visibility ahead of you — using the Lahaul–Spiti district administration advisory and the BRO position, not a long-range forecast made weeks earlier.

  1. Road open to civilian traffic all the way through, with no advisory pending? Manali is a go.
  2. Anything ambiguous — a system forecast, a partial restriction, one-way movement at Baralacha? Turn around and go back the way you came. You lose the loop, not the trip.
  3. If the bikes go Manali and the weather turns marginal, the SUV can carry both riders and the loop still ends safely. Agree this with everyone on day ten, not on the pass.

Whatever you decide: never sleep above Jispa.

Sarchu at 4,290 m is where this route actually hurts people — sub-zero, thin air, no heated rooms open in October, no help within hours. Jispa and Keylong have proper hotels running into November. Leh to Jispa in one day is long, and it is the correct kind of hard.

Sort these before you leave

  • Postpaid SIMs Prepaid connections from the rest of India will not activate in J&K or Ladakh. Every rider needs a postpaid number, arranged at home. This catches people out every season.
  • Heated rooms at Nubra and Pangong Tented camps fold up through October. Book permanent structures and get heating confirmed in writing, not over a phone call.
  • One medical oxygen cylinder in the SUV Portable cans are theatre. Carry a proper cylinder, plus a pulse oximeter, and take readings on the rest days so you know each person's baseline.
  • Fuel discipline Tandi to Karu is 365 km without a pump. Jerry cans in the SUV, filled at Tandi, and top the bikes up at Sarchu and Pang rather than running them low.
  • Inner Line Permits Needed for Nubra, Pangong and Khardung La. Do them on the Leh rest day, and carry printed copies — checkposts are not interested in your phone battery.
  • Cold rated for −10 °C, not −2 °C Leh nights sit near freezing by mid-October and Pangong goes well below. Heated grips earn their price on Chang La.

On the Srinagar side, budget for the Kashmir valley's own rhythms: convoy movement days, one-way timings at Zoji La, and occasional restrictions that have nothing to do with weather. Ask locally in Srinagar the evening before you ride, and keep the day-14 buffer intact for exactly this.
